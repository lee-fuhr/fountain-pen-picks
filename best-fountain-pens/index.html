The picks in detail

1. LAMY Safari Fountain Pen: the do-it-all default

Best overall
4.7out of 5

The LAMY Safari earns its reputation through thoughtful design and consistent quality. Its molded grip encourages a proper tripod hold, and the snap cap makes quick notes effortless. Owners report that the nibs are smooth with just a hint of feedback, and the pen is tough enough to survive a drop off a desk. For a first fountain pen or an everyday upgrade, it’s hard to beat.

Pros

  • Ergonomic, contoured grip that trains good handwriting habits
  • Snap-on cap very convenient for quick retrieval and capping
  • Wide range of vibrant colours and interchangeable nib sizes
  • Tough ABS plastic body holds up to daily commutes and student use

Cons

  • Ink windows are small and can be hard to read with some inks
  • Moulded grip does not suit everyone, lefties may need to test first
  • The steel nib, while reliable, lacks the bounce of gold options

Who it’s for

Writers who want a durable, ergonomic pen for daily longhand, school notes, or office jotting, it’s a true workhorse.

Who should skip it

You prefer a heavier metal body or a classic cigar-shaped pen; the Safari’s modern look and light weight won’t please everyone.

2. Pilot Metropolitan Fountain Pen: the polished metal alternative

Runner-up
4.6out of 5

The Pilot Metropolitan wraps a solid brass body in a classy, understated finish. It takes Pilot’s smooth steel nib, the same found in more expensive models, and pairs it with a converter that lets you use bottled ink. The slight heft gives it a premium feel that surprises people considering its price range. It is a close second and a favourite for those who want a bit of weight and retro style.

Pros

  • Durable brass body with enamel or a laqué finish resists scratches
  • Interchangeable nibs are smooth and wet out of the box
  • Includes a squeeze converter, so you can try bottled ink immediately
  • Classic silhouette and wide colour options look professional

Cons

  • The step-down from barrel to grip is sharp and bothers some writers
  • Cap is a friction-fit, it can loosen over time if not cared for
  • Slight weight may be fatiguing for very long writing sessions

Who it’s for

Professionals, students, and hobbyists who appreciate a slightly heavier pen with a classic look and the versatility of bottled ink.

Who should skip it

You prefer a lightweight body or need a snap cap for one-handed use; the Metropolitan’s twist-off cap and weight take some getting used to.

3. Kaweco Sport Fountain Pen: the compact pocket companion

Best value
4.5out of 5

The Kaweco Sport is a tiny, octagonal pen that posts deeply to become a full-length writer. Made from lightweight ABS (or aluminium in pricier versions), it punches above its size with a smooth steel nib that breaks in nicely. It’s an ideal grab-and-go pen for pockets, purses, or EDC kits. Just be aware that the screw cap takes a few seconds to remove, and the standard ink capacity is small due to the short body.

Pros

  • Extremely compact when capped, truly pocket-sized
  • Posting the cap creates a well-balanced full-length pen
  • Smooth German nib with decent line variation after some use
  • Available in many colours and limited editions for collectors

Cons

  • Screw cap is slower to open than a snap cap
  • Standard cartridge holds very little ink; mini converter is fiddly
  • Grip section is short and narrow, may not suit larger hands

Who it’s for

Anyone who carries a pen daily in a jean pocket, small bag, or notebook loop and wants something fashionable and functional.

Who should skip it

You write for long sessions without a break or need a large ink capacity; the Sport is better for quick notes than marathon letters.

4. Pilot Varsity Disposable Fountain Pen: the no-fuss budget starter

Best budget
4.3out of 5

The Pilot Varsity is a sealed, disposable fountain pen that costs less than a fancy cup of coffee and writes better than many pens three times its price. It requires no setup, no cartridge, and no cleaning, just uncap and go. The nib is surprisingly smooth for the price, and the ink typically lasts for months of moderate use. While it is not refillable or customizable, it is the perfect gateway into handwriting quality improvement without any financial commitment.

Pros

  • Extremely low-cost entry, no separate ink or converter needed
  • Smooth, reliable nib with no skipping or hard starts out of the box
  • Sealed design means it never dries out, even after weeks of disuse
  • Long ink reservoir offers surprisingly high total writing distance

Cons

  • Non-refillable, once the ink is gone, you throw the pen away
  • Only available in a few nib widths (mostly fine and medium)
  • Plastic body feels quite cheap compared to refillable options

Who it’s for

First time fountain pen users, travelers who need a throwaway pen, or anyone who wants to try fountain pen writing before investing in reusable gear.

Who should skip it

You care about sustainability, want to explore bottled inks, or prefer a pen that you can keep for years.

5. Waterman Hémisphère Fountain Pen: the accessible premium writer

Best premium
4.4out of 5

The Waterman Hémisphère brings French elegance and decades of heritage into a slim, metal-barrelled pen. It uses a smooth, fairly rigid steel nib that delivers a consistent line, and the lacquered finishes are refined without being ostentatious. It is an excellent step-up pen for someone who wants a nicer writing instrument without jumping to the true luxury tier. The main trade-offs are a slim grip (not for everyone) and a standard converter that requires extra care during filling.

Pros

  • Elegant, slim profile with a beautiful palette of lacquered finishes
  • Smooth steel nib with good flow, suitable for everyday office writing
  • Satisfying snap cap with a slightly soft close that feels premium
  • Backed by a company with a long reputation for quality and service

Cons

  • Grip is quite slim and polished, can become slippery after extended use
  • Nib is steel, not gold; some competitors at this price offer gold options
  • Converter is small and a bit fiddly to fill without mess

Who it’s for

Office workers and professionals who want a pen that looks and feels expensive but remains practical for daily use and signatures.

Who should skip it

You have larger hands, prefer a thicker grip, or need a gold nib for flex and softness; the Hémisphère stays firmly in the smooth-and-stiff category.

Best for specific needs

Best for everyday longhand and note-taking

If you write pages of journal entries, class notes, or meeting minutes every day, you want a pen that is comfortable, reliable, and not too precious to haul around. The LAMY Safari strikes this balance beautifully with its ergonomic grip and durable plastic body. It handles long writing sessions without cramping, and the snap cap means you can jot down a thought in a second. Our pick: LAMY Safari Fountain Pen.

Best for pocket carry and travel

A fountain pen that pokes out of your pocket invites broken caps or nibs. The Kaweco Sport looks like a fat stub when capped, sliding easily into a coin pocket or a small pen sleeve. Its screw cap is secure, and when posted it becomes a surprisingly comfortable full-size writer. It is ideal for urban sketchbook users or anyone who needs a pen on the go without dedicating a shirt pocket to it. Our pick: Kaweco Sport Fountain Pen.

Best for gift-giving on a budget

Fountain pens make thoughtful gifts, but quality can be hard to gauge. The Pilot Metropolitan packages a solid brass pen with a smooth nib and a classic design that looks twice its price. It comes in handsome gift-friendly colours and includes a converter, so the recipient can dabble in bottled inks. It is a gift that feels personal and lasting without breaking the bank. Our pick: Pilot Metropolitan Fountain Pen.

Our verdict

No single pen fits everyone, but the LAMY Safari stands out as the most versatile and forgiving choice for the widest range of people. If you want a metal body with a classic profile, nudge towards the Pilot Metropolitan. The Kaweco Sport is unbeatable for portability, the Pilot Varsity is the safest try-before-you-commit option, and the Waterman Hémisphère is a refined step up for those ready to invest in a daily companion. Whichever route you take, 2026 is a great year to start writing with less pressure and more pleasure.
